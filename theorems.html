Congruent Triangles: The Basics

What does it mean for two triangles to be congruent?

Two triangles are congruent if they have the same size and shape. Their corresponding sides have equal lengths, and their corresponding angles have equal measures.
△ABC ≅ △DEF
The order of the letters matters:
CorrespondenceMatching part
Vertex A ↔ D∠A ≅ ∠D
Vertex B ↔ E∠B ≅ ∠E
Vertex C ↔ F∠C ≅ ∠F
Side AB ↔ DEAB = DE
Side BC ↔ EFBC = EF
Side AC ↔ DFAC = DF
Important: Similar-looking triangles are not automatically congruent. You need enough given information to prove they have the same size and shape.
2

The Four Triangle Congruence Conditions

Know exactly which combination of sides and angles proves congruence.

SSS

Side-Side-Side

All three corresponding sides are equal.
3 sides = 3 sides

Example: AB = DE, BC = EF, AC = DF.

SAS

Side-Angle-Side

Two sides and the included angle between them are equal.
Side + included angle + side

The angle must be between the two known sides.

ASA

Angle-Side-Angle

Two angles and the included side between them are equal.
Angle + included side + angle

The known side lies between the two known angles.

AAS

Angle-Angle-Side

Two angles and a non-included side are equal.
Angle + angle + non-included side

The known side is not between the two known angles.

Memory trick: SSS = three sides; SAS = two sides with the angle between; ASA = two angles with the side between; AAS = two angles and a side outside them.
ConditionGiven informationProves congruence?
SSSThree pairs of equal sidesYes
SASTwo pairs of equal sides and included angleYes
ASATwo pairs of equal angles and included sideYes
AASTwo pairs of equal angles and a non-included sideYes
AAAThree pairs of equal anglesNo, proves similarity only
SSATwo sides and a non-included angleNot generally
3

Corresponding Parts and CPCTC

First prove the triangles congruent. Then use their matching parts.

CPCTC means Corresponding Parts of Congruent Triangles are Congruent. If △ABC ≅ △DEF, then corresponding sides and angles are equal.
△ABC ≅ △DEF ⟹ BC = EF and ∠A = ∠D
CPCTC is usually used after proving two triangles congruent. It is not itself a method for proving the triangles congruent.

Worked example

Given: △ABC ≅ △DEF, AB = 3x + 2, DE = 17.
  1. Because the triangles are congruent, AB corresponds to DE.
  2. By CPCTC, AB = DE.
  3. Substitute: 3x + 2 = 17.
  4. Subtract 2: 3x = 15.
  5. Divide by 3: x = 5.
Use the order of the triangle names to match corresponding vertices correctly.
4

How to Prove Triangles Congruent

A proof connects the given facts to a valid congruence condition.

  1. Read the given information. Identify equal sides, equal angles, shared sides, and parallel lines.
  2. Identify the two triangles. Write their names in the correct corresponding order.
  3. List three useful facts. Look for SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, or HL if the triangles are right triangles.
  4. State the congruence condition. Explain why the facts are sufficient.
  5. Use CPCTC. Once congruence is established, find any remaining equal sides or angles.

Worked proof: Shared side

Suppose AB = AD, BC = DC, and AC is shared.
StatementReason
AB = ADGiven
BC = DCGiven
AC = ACReflexive Property
△ABC ≅ △ADCSSS Congruence
Reflexive Property: Any segment or angle is congruent to itself. A shared side can count as one of the required equal sides.

Isosceles Triangle Theorem

Equal sides create equal opposite angles.

An isosceles triangle has at least two equal sides. The angles opposite those equal sides, called the base angles, are equal.
If AB = AC, then ∠B = ∠C
The converse is also true: if two angles in a triangle are equal, the sides opposite them are equal.

Worked example

In △ABC, AB = AC, ∠B = 2x + 10°, and ∠C = 50°.
  1. Equal sides mean equal base angles, so ∠B = ∠C.
  2. Set up: 2x + 10 = 50.
  3. Subtract 10: 2x = 40.
  4. Divide by 2: x = 20.
The vertex angle is opposite the base. The two base angles are opposite the equal sides.
6

Perpendicular Bisector Theorem

Points on a perpendicular bisector are equally distant from both endpoints.

A perpendicular bisector crosses a segment at its midpoint and forms a 90° angle.
If P is on the perpendicular bisector of AB, then PA = PB.
Converse: If PA = PB, then P lies on the perpendicular bisector of AB.

Worked example

Point P lies on the perpendicular bisector of AB. If PA = 4x − 3 and PB = 2x + 9, find x.
  1. Use the theorem: PA = PB.
  2. Set up: 4x − 3 = 2x + 9.
  3. Subtract 2x: 2x − 3 = 9.
  4. Add 3: 2x = 12.
  5. x = 6. Both distances equal 21 units.
7

Midline / Midsegment Theorem

A segment joining the midpoints of two sides of a triangle.

The triangle midsegment theorem states that a segment joining the midpoints of two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side and half as long.
MN ∥ BC and MN = ½ BC
If M is the midpoint of AB and N is the midpoint of AC:
  • AM = MB
  • AN = NC
  • MN is parallel to BC.
  • MN = BC ÷ 2, or BC = 2(MN).

Worked example

If MN is a midsegment and BC = 26 cm, find MN.
MN = ½(26) = 13 cm
If MN = 8 cm, then BC = 2(8) = 16 cm.
Do not confuse a triangle midsegment with a trapezoid midsegment. This lesson uses the triangle theorem.
8

Right Triangle Congruence Theorem (HL)

HL stands for Hypotenuse-Leg.

The HL theorem proves two right triangles congruent when their hypotenuses and one pair of corresponding legs are equal.
Right angle + equal hypotenuse + equal leg → HL
Remember: HL only applies to right triangles. The hypotenuse is always opposite the 90° angle and is the longest side.

Worked example

△ABC and △DEF are right triangles. AC and DF are hypotenuses, AC = DF, and AB = DE.
  1. Both triangles have a right angle.
  2. The hypotenuses are equal: AC = DF.
  3. One corresponding leg is equal: AB = DE.
  4. Therefore, △ABC ≅ △DEF by HL, provided the vertices are named in corresponding order.

Finding Missing Measurements

Translate geometric information into equations.

Example A: Corresponding sides

If △ABC ≅ △DEF, AB = 5x − 4, and DE = 21, find x.
  1. AB corresponds to DE, so AB = DE.
  2. 5x − 4 = 21.
  3. 5x = 25.
  4. x = 5.

Example B: Triangle angle sum

A triangle has angles 2x°, 3x°, and 85°. Find x.
  1. Interior angles sum to 180°.
  2. 2x + 3x + 85 = 180.
  3. 5x = 95.
  4. x = 19. The angles are 38°, 57°, and 85°.

Example C: Isosceles triangle

The base angles are (3x + 5)° and (5x − 15)°. Find x.
  1. Base angles are equal.
  2. 3x + 5 = 5x − 15.
  3. 20 = 2x.
  4. x = 10. Each base angle is 35°.

Example D: Midsegment

A triangle's midsegment is 3x + 2 and its parallel third side is 8x − 6. Find x.
  1. The midsegment is half the third side.
  2. 3x + 2 = ½(8x − 6).
  3. Multiply both sides by 2: 6x + 4 = 8x − 6.
  4. 10 = 2x, so x = 5.
  5. The midsegment is 17 units and the third side is 34 units.

Quick Review and Practice

Try each problem before revealing the answer.

1. Which condition uses three pairs of equal sides?

Answer: SSS.

2. Two sides and the angle between them are equal. Which condition?

Answer: SAS.

3. Why should CPCTC come after proving triangles congruent?

Answer: CPCTC states that corresponding parts of already-proven congruent triangles are congruent.

4. If AB = AC and ∠B = 42°, find ∠C.

Answer: ∠C = 42°, by the Isosceles Triangle Theorem.

5. P lies on the perpendicular bisector of AB. If PA = 3x + 4 and PB = 19, find x.

Answer: 3x + 4 = 19, so x = 5.

6. A triangle's third side is 32 cm. Find its midsegment length.

Answer: 32 ÷ 2 = 16 cm.

7. What two measurements, besides the right angles, are needed for HL?

Answer: Equal hypotenuses and one pair of equal corresponding legs.

8. Is AAA a triangle congruence condition?

Answer: No. AAA establishes similarity, not necessarily congruence.

9. If △ABC ≅ △PQR, which side corresponds to AC?

Answer: PR, because A ↔ P, B ↔ Q, and C ↔ R.

10. A midsegment is 11 cm. How long is the parallel third side?

Answer: 2 × 11 = 22 cm.
