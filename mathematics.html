Properties of Trapezoids and Kites

Learn the parts, properties, relationships, and important theorems involving trapezoids, isosceles trapezoids, and kites.

1. What is a Trapezoid?

Start by identifying its basic structure.

A trapezoid is a quadrilateral with at least one pair of parallel sides. The parallel sides are called the bases, while the non-parallel sides are called the legs.

Parts of a Trapezoid

2. Properties of Trapezoids

These properties belong specifically to a general trapezoid.

1

One Pair of Parallel Sides

A trapezoid has at least one pair of opposite sides that are parallel. These are called the bases.

2

Four Sides

A trapezoid is a quadrilateral, so it has four sides, four vertices, and four angles.

3

Consecutive Angles

The two interior angles along the same leg are supplementary, meaning they add up to 180°.

4

Height

The height is the perpendicular distance between the two bases.

Consecutive Interior Angles

3. Isosceles Trapezoid

A special type of trapezoid with additional properties.

An isosceles trapezoid is a trapezoid whose non-parallel sides, or legs, are congruent.

Isosceles Trapezoid

Properties of Isosceles Trapezoids

These are properties of the special isosceles trapezoid, not necessarily every trapezoid.

1

Congruent Legs

The two non-parallel sides, called the legs, are congruent.

AD ≅ BC

2

Congruent Base Angles

The angles sharing the same base are congruent.

∠A ≅ ∠B

∠D ≅ ∠C

3

Supplementary Consecutive Angles

Angles along the same leg are supplementary and add up to 180°.

4

Congruent Diagonals

The diagonals of an isosceles trapezoid are congruent.

AC ≅ BD

Congruent Diagonals

4. Trapezoid Midsegment

A segment connecting the midpoints of the two legs.

The midsegment of a trapezoid connects the midpoints of its legs. It is parallel to both bases and its length is half the sum of the two bases.

Midsegment = (Base₁ + Base₂) ÷ 2
Trapezoid Midsegment
Example

A trapezoid has bases measuring 12 cm and 20 cm. Find the length of its midsegment.

1

Add the two bases: 12 + 20 = 32

2

Divide by 2: 32 ÷ 2 = 16

3

Therefore, the midsegment is 16 cm.

5. Kite

A quadrilateral with two distinct pairs of adjacent congruent sides.

A kite is a quadrilateral with two pairs of adjacent congruent sides. The congruent sides are next to each other, not opposite each other.

Parts of a Kite

6. Properties of Kites

Keep these properties separate from trapezoid properties.

1

Two Pairs of Adjacent Congruent Sides

A kite has two pairs of sides that are congruent and adjacent.

AB ≅ AD

CB ≅ CD

2

One Pair of Opposite Congruent Angles

A kite has one pair of opposite angles that are congruent.

∠B ≅ ∠D

3

Perpendicular Diagonals

The diagonals of a kite intersect at right angles.

AC ⟂ BD

4

One Diagonal Bisects the Other

One diagonal divides the other diagonal into two congruent segments.

5

Angle Bisector

One diagonal bisects a pair of opposite angles of the kite.

6

Important Reminder

A kite does not generally have opposite congruent sides. Its congruent sides are adjacent.

Kite Diagonals

7. Kite: What to Look For

Use the shape's appearance to identify its properties.

Look at the sides: Find two pairs of adjacent congruent sides.
Look at the angles: One pair of opposite angles is congruent.
Look at the diagonals: They intersect perpendicularly.
Look at the diagonal: One diagonal bisects the other.

8. Area of a Trapezoid

Another important formula to remember.

To find the area of a trapezoid, use the two bases and the height.

A = ½(b₁ + b₂)h

Where: b₁ = first base, b₂ = second base, and h = height.

9. How to Solve Geometry Problems

A simple process to follow when given a diagram.

1

Identify the shape. Is it a trapezoid, isosceles trapezoid, or kite?

2

Mark the known information. Look for parallel, congruent, or perpendicular markings.

3

Choose the correct property. Do not use a kite property on a trapezoid.

4

Set up the relationship. For example, supplementary angles add to 180°.

5

Solve and check. Make sure the answer makes sense with the diagram.

12. Quick Review

The most important things to remember before a quiz.

Trapezoid: Has at least one pair of parallel sides.
Trapezoid angles: Consecutive interior angles along a leg add to 180°.
Isosceles trapezoid: Its legs are congruent.
Isosceles trapezoid: Its base angles are congruent.
Isosceles trapezoid: Its diagonals are congruent.
Kite: Has two pairs of adjacent congruent sides.
Kite: Has one pair of opposite congruent angles.
Kite: Its diagonals are perpendicular.
Kite: One diagonal bisects the other.
Important: A kite's congruent sides are adjacent, not generally opposite.
