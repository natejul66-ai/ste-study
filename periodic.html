Introduction

What Is the Periodic Table?

The periodic table is an organized arrangement of the chemical elements according to their atomic numbers, electron configurations, and recurring chemical properties. It contains all 118 currently recognized elements.

How the Table Is Organized

The periodic table contains 7 periods arranged horizontally and 18 groups arranged vertically. Elements in the same group generally have similar valence-electron configurations and therefore often show similar chemical behavior.

Remember: Atomic number increases from left to right across a period and generally increases from top to bottom through the table.

Periodic Table Groups

Learn what each major group represents and how to recognize it.

1

Alkali Metals

Very reactive metals with one valence electron. Hydrogen is positioned here but is a nonmetal.

2

Alkaline Earth Metals

Reactive metals with two valence electrons.

3–12

Transition Metals

The central block of metals containing elements such as iron, copper, silver, gold, and mercury.

13

Boron Group

Contains boron, aluminum, gallium, indium, thallium, and nihonium.

14

Carbon Group

Contains carbon, silicon, germanium, tin, lead, and flerovium.

15

Nitrogen Group

Also called the pnictogens. Nitrogen and phosphorus are important members of this group.

16

Oxygen Group

Also called the chalcogens. Oxygen and sulfur are common members.

17

Halogens

Highly reactive nonmetals that commonly form salts with metals.

18

Noble Gases

Very unreactive gases with filled outer electron shells.

f

Lanthanides and Actinides

The two rows normally displayed below the main table. They belong to periods 6 and 7 respectively.

How to Memorize the Periodic Table

Use patterns, chunks, symbols, and repetition instead of trying to memorize all 118 elements at once.

1. Learn in Chunks

Divide the periodic table into smaller sections. Start with the first 20 elements, then learn the major groups and transition-metal rows.

First 20: H, He, Li, Be, B, C, N, O, F, Ne, Na, Mg, Al, Si, P, S, Cl, Ar, K, Ca

2. Memorize Symbols by Sound

Say the symbols aloud in sequence. This helps connect the visual symbol with its pronunciation.

Group 1: H → Li → Na → K → Rb → Cs → Fr

Mnemonic: Happy Little Nannies Keep Rabbits Calm Forever

3. Use Group Patterns

Elements in the same group have related electron configurations and often show similar properties.

Group 17: F → Cl → Br → I → At → Ts

Funny Clowns Bring Ice At Tuesday School

4. Learn Periods as Sequences

Instead of randomly memorizing symbols, memorize each horizontal period as a sequence.

Period 2: Li → Be → B → C → N → O → F → Ne

Little Betty Bought Cookies Near Orange Flowers Nearby

5. Watch for Strange Symbols

Some symbols do not directly match the English name. These deserve extra attention.

Important examples: Na = Sodium
K = Potassium
Fe = Iron
Cu = Copper
Ag = Silver
Au = Gold
Hg = Mercury
Pb = Lead

6. Practice From a Blank Table

After studying the table, remove the answers and try rebuilding it from memory. This is more effective than repeatedly looking at the completed table.

Study cycle: Look → Cover → Recall → Check → Repeat

Memorizing Atomic Masses

Learn the common classroom values first, then work toward the more precise atomic masses.

Do Not Memorize 118 Decimal Numbers at Once

Start with rounded values commonly used in chemistry calculations. Once those are familiar, gradually learn more precise values for elements that frequently appear in your exercises.

Step 1

Memorize the first 20 elements and their approximate atomic masses.

Step 2

Learn the masses of frequently used elements such as Na, Mg, Al, Cl, K, Ca, Fe, Cu, Zn, Ag, and Au.

Important Atomic Mass Rule

Atomic mass is not the same thing as atomic number. The atomic number tells you the number of protons in an atom, while atomic mass represents the weighted average mass of the element's naturally occurring isotopes.

Example: Sodium has atomic number 11 but an atomic mass of about 22.99, commonly rounded to 23.0 for calculations.

Quick Review

Key facts to remember before taking the final test.

118 recognized chemical elements are currently included in the periodic table.
18 groups run vertically.
7 periods run horizontally.
Group 1 contains the alkali metals, with hydrogen treated separately as a nonmetal.
Group 17 contains the halogens.
Group 18 contains the noble gases.
Lanthanides occupy elements 57–71.
Actinides occupy elements 89–103.
Atomic number identifies the element and equals the number of protons in a neutral atom.
Atomic mass is the weighted average mass of an element's naturally occurring isotopes.
