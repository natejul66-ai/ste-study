Molarity: Concentration of Solutions

Learn how molarity describes the concentration of a solution and how to calculate molarity, moles, volume, mass, dilution, and solution stoichiometry.

1. What Is Molarity?

The basic measurement of solution concentration.

Molarity is the number of moles of solute dissolved per liter of solution.

M = n / V M = molarity, n = moles of solute, V = volume of solution in liters

Molarity is commonly expressed in mol/L, which is also written as M.

Important: Molarity uses the volume of the entire solution, not simply the volume of the solvent.

3. Converting mL to L

Molarity calculations require volume in liters.

1000 mL = 1 L
Example

Convert 250 mL to liters.

250 mL ÷ 1000 = 0.250 L

Answer: 0.250 L

Common mistake: Never directly use milliliters in the molarity formula. Convert mL to L first.

4. Calculating Molarity from Moles

When the number of moles and solution volume are given.

M = n / V
Example

Find the molarity of a solution containing 0.50 mol NaCl in 2.0 L of solution.

M = 0.50 mol ÷ 2.0 L

M = 0.25 M

5. Mass → Moles → Molarity

Finding molarity when the mass of solute is given.

Mass grams
→
Moles n = mass / MM
→
Molarity M = n / V

Step 1: Convert Mass to Moles

n = mass / molar mass

Step 2: Calculate Molarity

M = n / V
Example: NaCl

Calculate the molarity of a solution made by dissolving 5.85 g NaCl in 500 mL of solution.

Molar mass of NaCl ≈ 58.5 g/mol

n = 5.85 g ÷ 58.5 g/mol

n = 0.100 mol

500 mL = 0.500 L

M = 0.100 mol ÷ 0.500 L

M = 0.200 M

6. Finding Mass from Molarity

Work backward from concentration to grams.

M × V Find moles
→
n × MM Find grams
n = MV Then use mass = n × molar mass.
Example: KOH

How many grams of KOH are needed to prepare 250 mL of 0.500 M KOH?

V = 250 mL = 0.250 L

n = MV

n = (0.500)(0.250) = 0.125 mol

Molar mass of KOH ≈ 56.1 g/mol

mass = 0.125 × 56.1

mass ≈ 7.01 g KOH

7. Dilution

Reducing concentration by adding solvent.

M₁V₁ = M₂V₂ M₁ and V₁ are the initial concentration and volume. M₂ and V₂ are the final values.
Concentrated Solution → Diluted Solution
Concentrated
→
Diluted
Example

How much 6.0 M HCl is needed to prepare 250 mL of 1.0 M HCl?

M₁V₁ = M₂V₂

(6.0)(V₁) = (1.0)(250 mL)

V₁ = 250 ÷ 6.0

V₁ = 41.7 mL

8. Mixing Solutions

Combining solutions requires tracking total moles and volume.

ntotal = n₁ + n₂
Mfinal = ntotal / Vtotal

When solutions containing the same solute are mixed, determine the total amount of solute and divide it by the total solution volume.

Example Reaction

The coefficients in a balanced chemical equation provide the mole ratios needed for stoichiometric calculations.

10. Solution Stoichiometry

A systematic method for solving reaction problems involving solutions.

  1. Balance the chemical equation.
  2. Convert solution volumes to liters.
  3. Use M × V to find moles.
  4. Apply the mole ratio from the balanced equation.
  5. Convert the resulting moles into the quantity requested.
Remember: Chemical equations must be balanced before using their coefficients as mole ratios.

11. Common Mistakes

Check these before submitting your answer.

01. Forgetting to convert mL to L.
02. Using solvent volume instead of total solution volume.
03. Mixing up moles and molarity.
04. Using an incorrect molar mass.
05. Forgetting to balance a chemical equation before using mole ratios.
06. Using the wrong dilution equation or pairing the wrong values.
