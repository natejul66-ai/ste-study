Earth's Interior and Seismic Waves

Learn how seismic waves travel, how scientists study Earth's hidden layers, what discontinuities reveal about the planet, and how earthquake early warning and preparedness help protect communities in the Philippines.

1. What Are Seismic Waves?

Earthquakes release energy that travels through Earth and along its surface.

Seismic waves are waves of energy produced by earthquakes, volcanic activity, explosions, or other disturbances. Scientists study their arrival times, speeds, and paths to investigate Earth's interior.

Body Waves

Travel through Earth's interior. The two main types are P-waves and S-waves.

Surface Waves

Travel along Earth's surface. They often produce strong ground motion and can cause significant damage.

Two Main Paths of Seismic Waves

2. P-Waves and S-Waves

Both are body waves, but they move differently through materials.

Primary Wave

P-Waves

P-waves are the fastest seismic body waves, so they are generally the first to arrive at a seismic station.


Motion: Particles vibrate parallel to the direction of travel.

Materials: Solids, liquids, and gases.

Other name: Compressional or longitudinal waves.

Secondary Wave

S-Waves

S-waves travel more slowly than P-waves and usually arrive second. They cannot travel through liquids.


Motion: Particles vibrate perpendicular to the direction of travel.

Materials: Solids only.

Other name: Shear or transverse waves.

3. Seismographs and Seismograms

One detects and records ground motion; the other is the recorded result.

Seismograph

A seismograph is an instrument used to detect and record ground motion caused by seismic waves. Modern instruments commonly use electronic sensors and digital recording systems.

Seismogram

A seismogram is the recorded trace or data showing ground motion over time. Scientists examine it to identify wave arrivals and analyze an earthquake.

Example of a Seismogram

Illustrative trace only; real seismograms vary by earthquake, distance, and instrument.

How to Read a Seismogram

1

Identify the first clear arrival, usually the P-wave.

2

Identify the later S-wave arrival, if recorded.

3

Measure the time difference between the P- and S-wave arrivals.

4

Use the arrival-time difference with travel-time information to estimate the station's distance from the earthquake source.

4. How Seismic Waves Reveal Earth's Hidden Layers

Wave speed, direction, and missing arrivals reveal what lies beneath the surface.

Changes in Speed

Seismic waves change speed when they pass through materials with different properties.

Refraction

Waves can bend when their speed changes as they enter another layer.

Reflection

Some wave energy bounces off boundaries between layers.

Shadow Zones

Regions where certain direct seismic waves are not detected, helping scientists infer internal boundaries.

Wave Paths Inside Earth

What the Evidence Tells Scientists

1

P- and S-wave speeds vary with the properties of the material they pass through.

2

S-waves are not transmitted through Earth's liquid outer core.

3

P-waves pass through the outer core but are strongly refracted, producing a P-wave shadow zone.

4

Wave patterns and other geophysical evidence help scientists infer the structure and physical state of Earth's layers.

Important: The absence of direct S-waves through the outer core is key evidence that the outer core is liquid. P-wave behavior also supports this conclusion.

5. Discontinuities Inside Earth

Discontinuities are boundaries where seismic-wave behavior changes because the material changes.

Major Internal Boundaries

Mohorovičić Discontinuity (Moho)

The Moho is the boundary between Earth's crust and mantle. Seismic-wave speeds generally increase as waves enter the mantle because the rocks have different physical properties.

Gutenberg Discontinuity

The Gutenberg discontinuity is the boundary between the mantle and the liquid outer core, at roughly 2,900 kilometers below Earth's surface. S-waves cannot travel through the liquid outer core, and P-waves change speed and direction there.

Lehmann Discontinuity

The inner-core boundary, often associated with Inge Lehmann's discovery, separates the liquid outer core from the solid inner core at roughly 5,150 kilometers depth.

6. Earthquake Early Warning

Early warning can provide a short amount of time to take protective action.

An earthquake early warning system detects an earthquake that has already begun and sends alerts to places that may experience strong shaking. It uses sensor data and rapid processing to provide warning before stronger shaking arrives, where time and system coverage allow.

How Early Warning Works

Early Warning

Issued after an earthquake starts, before strong shaking reaches a location when enough time is available.

Earthquake Prediction

Would identify the exact time, location, and magnitude in advance. Reliable exact prediction is not currently possible.

Remember: An alert is not a guarantee that shaking will follow, and the absence of an alert does not mean an earthquake is not happening. Always follow official local guidance.

7. Earthquake Preparedness in the Philippines

Know what to do before, during, and after an earthquake.

Before an Earthquake

Prepare and Reduce Hazards

Identify safe spots away from windows and heavy objects.
Secure shelves, cabinets, appliances, and hanging objects.
Prepare an emergency kit with water, food, a flashlight, a radio, batteries, medicines, and first-aid supplies.
Make a family communication and reunion plan.
Know evacuation routes and local emergency instructions.
During an Earthquake

Drop, Cover, and Hold On

The Three Protective Actions
DROP: Get down on your hands and knees so the shaking is less likely to knock you over.
COVER: Protect your head and neck under sturdy furniture. If there is no shelter, get low and cover your head and neck with your arms.
HOLD ON: Hold your shelter until the shaking stops and be ready to move with it.
If you are indoors, stay inside during the shaking. Stay away from windows and do not rush toward exits. If you are outside, move to an open area away from buildings, trees, power lines, and other hazards.
After an Earthquake

Stay Alert and Check for Hazards

Expect aftershocks and be ready to Drop, Cover, and Hold On again.
Check yourself and others for injuries when it is safe; give first aid if trained.
Avoid damaged buildings, fallen power lines, and unsafe roads.
If you smell gas or suspect a leak, leave the area and report it safely. Do not operate electrical switches in the affected area.
Follow announcements from PHIVOLCS, local authorities, and emergency responders.
If you are in a coastal area and feel strong or long earthquake shaking, move inland or to higher ground when safe and follow official tsunami evacuation instructions.

8. Philippine Earthquake Safety Agencies

Know which organizations provide scientific information and emergency support.

PHIVOLCS

The Philippine Institute of Volcanology and Seismology monitors earthquakes and volcanoes and provides scientific information, hazard maps, and public advisories.

9. Putting the Concepts Together

How scientists move from earthquake recordings to an understanding of Earth's interior.

1

An earthquake releases energy that travels as seismic waves.

2

Seismographs detect and record ground motion, producing seismograms.

3

Scientists compare P-wave and S-wave arrival times and examine their paths.

4

Changes in wave speed, refraction, and shadow zones reveal boundaries and physical differences inside Earth.

5

Scientists use this knowledge to understand earthquakes and improve hazard assessment and preparedness.

10. Quick Review

Memorize these key facts before your assessment.

Seismic waves: Energy waves produced by earthquakes and other disturbances.
Body waves: Travel through Earth's interior.
Surface waves: Travel along Earth's surface.
P-waves: Faster compressional waves that can travel through solids, liquids, and gases.
S-waves: Shear waves that travel through solids but not liquids or gases.
Seismograph: Instrument that detects and records ground motion.
Seismogram: The recorded trace or data from a seismic recording.
Moho: Boundary between the crust and mantle.
Gutenberg discontinuity: Boundary between the mantle and liquid outer core.
Shadow zones: Regions where certain direct seismic waves are not detected.
Early warning: Detects an earthquake after it begins and may provide time to take protective action.
Earthquake safety: Drop, Cover, and Hold On during shaking.
PHIVOLCS: Philippine agency providing earthquake and volcano monitoring and scientific advisories.
