Definition of Terms

Learn what the Definition of Terms section is for, the difference between conceptual and operational definitions, and how to format this section correctly in a research paper.

Not completed

1. What Is the "Definition of Terms" Section?

Making sure every reader understands your key words the same way.

The Definition of Terms is a section of a research paper — usually near the end of Chapter 1 — that clarifies the meaning of important, technical, or study-specific words so that anyone reading the paper interprets them the same way the researcher intended.

Why It Matters

Words like "performance," "engagement," or "stress" mean different things to different people. Without a clear definition, readers might misunderstand your findings or conclusions.

2. Where It Appears in a Research Paper

Usually the last part of the introduction chapter.

In most Philippine research formats, Definition of Terms comes at the end of Chapter 1 (Introduction), right after the Scope and Limitations, and before Chapter 2 (Review of Related Literature).

3. Conceptual vs. Operational Definition

The single most important distinction in this section.

C

Conceptual Definition: the general, theoretical meaning of a term, usually taken from a dictionary, a textbook, or another author's work — and must be cited.

O

Operational Definition: how the term is specifically used, measured, or observed in this particular study.

Example

Term: Academic Performance
Conceptual — "the extent to which a student has achieved educational goals" (cited from a source).
Operational — "in this study, refers to the student's general weighted average (GWA) for the first semester."

4. Which Terms Should You Define?

Not every word in your title needs an entry.

Include

Technical or specialized words, words used in a special way in your study, and any variable named in your title or research questions.

Skip

Common, everyday words that every reader already understands the same way (e.g., "student," "school," "table") — defining these makes the section look padded.

5. Format and Structure

Keep it clean, consistent, and easy to scan.

1
List terms in alphabetical order.
2
Bold or underline the term itself.
3
Give the conceptual definition first (with a citation), then the operational definition.
4
Keep each definition to one or two sentences.

6. Sample Entries

A quick model you can follow for your own paper.

Term Conceptual Definition Operational Definition
Online Learning Education delivered through the internet, cited from literature Use of the school's LMS during the school year of the study
Stress A state of mental or emotional strain, cited from literature Score on the stress-scale questionnaire used in this study
Respondents Individuals who provide data for a study The Grade 10 students of the school surveyed in this research

7. Common Mistakes

Easy ways to lose points on this section.

Defining ordinary words that need no clarification.
Copying a dictionary definition only, with no operational definition tied to the actual study.
Forgetting the citation for a conceptual definition taken from another source.
Inconsistent terms — using a word one way here and a different way later in the paper.

8. Quick Review

The most important things to remember before a quiz.

Definition of Terms: clarifies key words so every reader understands them the same way.
Location: end of Chapter 1, before the Review of Related Literature.
Conceptual definition: general/theoretical meaning, cited.
Operational definition: how the term is used/measured in this specific study.
Only define: technical, specialized, or study-specific terms.
Order: alphabetical, term in bold.
